People sometimes ask whether online fellowship is real fellowship. It's a fair question, and we've asked it ourselves.

## What the Bible asks of us

Hebrews tells us to "consider one another in order to stir up love and good works," and not to forsake "the assembling of ourselves together." The heart of that command is not a building. It is people paying attention to each other, encouraging each other, and meeting together regularly.

For many in our fellowship, distance, work schedules, health or family responsibilities make a weekly in-person meeting impossible. For them, a call on Tuesday and Thursday evening is not a lesser option. It is the option that lets them keep that command.

## What makes it real

A screen can be a doorway or a wall. What makes the difference is what we do on the other side of it:

- **We know each other's names.** And we use them, in prayer and in conversation.
- **We pray for specific things.** Not "bless everyone," but this job interview, that hospital appointment.
- **We follow up.** Someone who shares a need on Tuesday hears from someone else before Thursday.
- **We meet in person when we can.** Online fellowship isn't a reason never to meet; it often becomes the reason we do.

## A doorway, not a destination

We don't think the screen is the goal. Jesus came in the flesh, and the church is a body. But we have seen God use these calls to draw people in from a dozen countries, to carry people through illness and loss, and to grow real friendships.

If you have been watching from a distance, consider this your invitation to step through the doorway. Join us live, say hello in the chat, and let us get to know you.
