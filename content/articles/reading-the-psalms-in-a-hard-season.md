About a third of the Psalms are laments: prayers of complaint, grief, confusion and longing. That alone should tell us something. God gave His people words for their worst days, and He put them right in the middle of their songbook.

## Lament is honest

The psalmists don't tidy up their feelings before they pray. "My tears have been my food day and night," says Psalm 42. "Why have You forgotten me?" asks Psalm 42 again, a few verses later.

That honesty isn't a failure of faith. It is faith. You only complain to someone you believe can do something about it.

## Lament turns

Most laments follow a pattern. They begin with the pain, move into a request, and then, often suddenly, turn toward trust. Psalm 42 speaks to itself:

> Why are you cast down, O my soul? … Hope in God, for I shall yet praise Him.

The circumstances haven't changed yet. But the psalmist has reminded his own heart who God is.

## How to read them when you're hurting

1. **Read slowly.** One psalm a day is plenty.
2. **Underline the lines you could have written.** Let them be your prayer.
3. **Notice the turn.** Where does the psalm move from pain toward trust? You don't have to be there yet. But it is good to see that the road goes somewhere.
4. **Pray it with others.** Lament was sung together. Bring your hard season into the fellowship and let others hold the hope when you can't.

Some seasons don't end quickly. The Psalms don't pretend they do. But they promise that even the darkest night is lived in the presence of a God who hears.
