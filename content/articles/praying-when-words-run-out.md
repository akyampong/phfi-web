There are weeks when prayer comes easily. The words are there, the thanks is real, and the requests line up neatly. And then there are the other weeks: the diagnosis, the long silence after an argument, the grief that sits on the chest like a weight. We bow our heads and find that we have nothing to say.

If that is where you are, you are in good company.

## The Spirit prays when we can't

Paul doesn't pretend prayer is always easy. He writes that "we do not know what we should pray for as we ought," and then gives one of the most comforting promises in the New Testament: the Spirit Himself intercedes for us "with groanings which cannot be uttered."

A groan is not eloquent. It isn't organised into points. But it is honest, and God hears it as prayer. On the days when all you can manage is a sigh in His direction, that sigh is not wasted.

## Borrow the Psalms

For centuries, believers have found that when their own words run dry, the Psalms give them words to borrow. Psalm 13 begins, "How long, O Lord?" Psalm 88 ends in darkness. Psalm 23 walks through the valley of the shadow of death and comes out the other side.

You can pray them as they are. Read a psalm slowly, out loud if you can, and let it become your own. You will often find that the psalmist has already said what you feel but couldn't name.

## Small prayers count

When words run out, try praying smaller:

- **One line, many times.** "Lord, have mercy." "Help me." "I trust You."
- **Name a person and stop.** Say their name before God and leave it there.
- **Let someone pray for you.** This is part of what fellowship is for. Send a prayer request, or ask someone on a Tuesday night to carry it with you.

> Prayer is not a performance for God. It is a child turning toward a Father who already knows.

The words will come back. Until they do, keep turning toward Him. He is listening even to the silence.
