Hospitality in the Bible is more than having people round for dinner. The word Paul uses in Romans 12 means, quite literally, love of strangers. It's about making room for people who aren't yet part of us.

That raises a question for a fellowship that mostly meets online: how do you welcome someone when there's no front door?

## Notice the newcomer

The first act of hospitality is noticing. When a new name appears in the chat or on the call, greet them by name. Ask where they're joining from. It's a small thing that tells someone they've been seen.

## Make the way in clear

Newcomers don't know our rhythms yet. Explain what's happening, share the meeting times, point them to the prayer request page. Don't assume they know what "Jericho Hour" means.

## Follow up

Real welcome continues after the call ends. A short message during the week can turn a visitor into a friend.

## Open the actual door, too

Online fellowship often leads to real tables. When you can, invite people into your home, or meet them for coffee. The screen is where many friendships begin; it doesn't have to be where they stay.

Practise hospitality, Paul says. Practice suggests repetition, getting better over time. Let's become a fellowship known for its welcome.
